# Development Log

## The Unpicker's entrance, gentler grey, and a few fixes
- The opening now gives the Unpicker his own shot: the camera goes up to the crack in the lid
  and pushes in slowly as he lowers himself through, upside down, lit violet so he stands
  out, before the Fray pours out past him. (Before, the camera never looked at him.) The
  opening is three seconds longer.
- The grey near the Fray is much gentler: at its strongest it takes away about half the
  colour (it used to drain nearly all of it), and only when they're quite close.
- Near the Fray the music also goes a little muffled and quieter, easing back as you move away.
- Two hay bales of yarn on the way to the Whispering Wood had ended up in its gate at the top of
  the ravine steps, blocking it: moved clear.
- A hedge south of town ran out over the canal: it stops at the railing now.
- Toy animals no longer walk off edges onto thin air: they check there's ground under their
  next step (so they won't wander over the ravine or the canal), and one that does end up
  with nothing under it goes back.

## The Fray, redesigned: things from the Underside, and the Unpicker
- New story for the Fray: it isn't from Patch's world. It comes from the Underside, the back
  of the cloth, where nothing has ever had a colour. Its creatures eat colour.
- All four creatures have new, uncanny bodies (my own designs, inspired by museum photos of
  furry long-legged creature sculptures): ash-grey fur, legs that are too long with feet that
  are too big, eyes out on stalks that sway and blink out of time with each other, mouths
  that are too wide with too few teeth, and a pink tongue (the only colour on them).
  - Snarling: a fur dome striding on two long scaly legs.
  - Tangle Hopper: a striped sock on one fat leg, its mouth on top.
  - Static Moth: a one-eyed puff with feelers, ragged wings and dangling legs.
  - Static Spitter: nearly all mouth, eyes up on top, hands for feet.
  Knots of the Fray have eye stalks too.
- The world goes grey where they go: each creature leaves grey blots on the ground where it
  walks (they fade a while after it moves on), and the nearer Patch is to any of them the
  more the whole picture drains towards grey. Unravel one and its blots shrink away in a
  burst of coloured pompoms, with a little flash of extra colour.
- The Unpicker, the one behind the Fray: five and a half metres tall, thin as a needle, a
  thimble for a hat, a seam ripper for a staff, a mouth sewn shut, five eyes on stalks, and
  the stolen colours hanging from his coat in glowing jars. In the opening he lowers himself
  head-first through the crack in the lid to look at the town before sending the Fray in.
  During the level he stands on rooftops and hilltops a long way off, watching; get near and
  he folds away like an umbrella and turns up somewhere else later. The Storyteller notices
  him the first two times. He stops coming once the town is mended.
- Removed the old tin-beetle Snarling.

## Band quests with a twist, a wedding that grows, and the Pod's ring of light
- The lute: Captain Buttonbeard, a pirate on top of Spindle Hill, has the string and thinks
  "lute" means "loot". He'll only swap it for his own treasure, buried under a stitched red X
  in the sandpit by his wrecked toy boat (out east, on the way to the Sky Hoops). Dig it up,
  go back and he hands the string over. You can find the treasure before you ever meet him.
- The piper: Wisp is scared of everything. Get close and he squeaks and flaps off to the next
  cloud up. There's now a staircase of four clouds above the weathervane, each with a puff of
  steam to ride to the next, and he only calms down when he runs out of sky.
- The trumpet: the valve dew only gathers while all three dye taps are running. Two are
  inside the Dye Works (first floor and top floor); the third is the intake in the canal wall,
  down at the waterline. Each tap starts its colour flowing and speeds up the waterwheel.
- Five lost wedding presents are hidden in far corners (out west by the campsite, on the
  third cloud, adrift in the canal, down in the ravine, the north-east corner). Each adds
  something to the wedding: fireworks, confetti cannons, balloons, paper doves, bunting.
  Lady Lacewing keeps count and gives a clue to the next one.
- The more secrets found overall (presents, Heartstrings, golden buttons, marbles), the more
  guests come to the wedding and the thicker the confetti. Presents found after the wedding
  still turn up in the square, and the fireworks go off now and then over the married town.
- The Pod: the three aurora curtains are now one ring of 96 glowing bars right round the Pod.
  It breathes slowly on its own; with the controller in hand it becomes the song's soundwave
  (each bar follows part of the music, with a ripple on every beat) and its colours drift
  round like the northern lights: green, teal, blue, violet, rose.
- The Pod's chime is much softer and lower, and the little surprise noises are quieter and
  about half as often.
- Fixed the Toybox Town band sounding out of time. A layer that was "off" sat at zero volume,
  and the sound engine doesn't really play silent sounds, so it came back in slightly late.
  Quiet layers now stay at a volume too low to hear, every layer starts on one audio tick,
  and the game re-lines them up if they ever slip. The flute and horns also start their notes
  a hair early (they take a moment to speak), and the plucked strings sit on the same beat
  grid as the harp.

## Fixed: the Pod vanishing
- After pressing Play a second time without scripts reloading, Unity had sometimes thrown

## Builds on other computers, and running faster
- Runs much faster: the level's still decorations (tens of thousands of little shapes) are now
  merged into a few hundred meshes when a level loads, one per material per 32 m patch of the
  map. The picture is the same; the computer has far less to do every frame. Anything a script
  moves, recolours, hides or picks up (and everything under it) is found automatically and kept
  separate, as are see-through things. The optimiser also uses its own cube and cylinder
  meshes, so they can always be merged in a built game.
- New Graphics setting (Settings > Graphics: Fast / Balanced / Best). The first time the game
  runs it picks one to suit the computer's graphics card. Fast and Balanced draw at a slightly
  lower resolution with FSR upscaling, less anti-aliasing, closer and simpler shadows, and draw
  small decorations a little less far.
- Builds for other computers: Windows builds now use DirectX 11 first (much wider graphics card
  and driver support; DirectX 12 stays as a fallback), and the fog shaders are always kept
  (the haze is switched on from code, so Unity was leaving it out of builds).
- Every build gets a build number, shown small in the corner of the title screen, and is
  zipped automatically next to the build folder as "Patchwork Build N.zip", ready to send.
- A built game writes "Patchwork Log.txt" next to Patchwork.exe: the computer's graphics card
  and settings, and any warnings or errors. Send that file if something looks wrong.
- The map can take you straight back to where the Pod landed ("Travel back to the Pod"), and
  its layout is tidied so the tape measure and prompts don't overlap the edge.

## Builds look the same as the editor
- Built games were running with no post-processing at all (no bloom, colour grading, warm and
  cool tint or vignette), so they looked flat compared with Play mode. The Player.log showed
  why: URP stripped some post-processing shaders it thought were unused (depth of field,
  Panini projection), and a build missing any of them turns post-processing off entirely. The
  look is made from Volume profiles built in code, so URP can't see what's used. Turned off
  "strip unused post-processing" in the URP global settings, and added a pre-build check
  (`PatchworkBuildChecks`) that turns it back off before every build if it ever gets reset.
